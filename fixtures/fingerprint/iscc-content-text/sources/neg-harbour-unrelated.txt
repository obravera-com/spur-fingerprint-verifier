Engineers at Greywater Haven have begun a two-year programme to raise the height of the town's sea wall by almost a metre, the first major change to the defences since they were built in the 1960s. The council approved the scheme in spring after a study warned that storm surges could overtop the existing wall within a decade.

The work will be carried out in sections so that the promenade stays open for most of the summer season. Traders on the seafront had objected to an earlier plan that would have closed the whole length at once, and the council agreed to phase the contract after a petition gathered several thousand signatures.

Contractors will start at the southern end near the lifeboat station, where the wall is lowest, and move north through the winter. Each section is expected to take about six weeks, with the new parapet cast in place behind temporary steel sheeting.

The lifeboat crew will operate from a temporary slipway during the first phase. Fishing boats using the small inner harbour are not affected, although the harbourmaster has warned of noise and dust during working hours.

Funding comes from a national coastal resilience grant and the council's capital budget. Councillors said the scheme would protect around four hundred homes and the town's main shopping street from flooding.
