The harbour authority at Port Meridian confirmed on Tuesday that the eastern breakwater will close to all traffic for eleven weeks from the first of October, while engineers replace the concrete armour units damaged in last winter's storms. The closure affects the fishing fleet, the ferry to Salt Island and the small leisure marina behind the breakwater.

Officials said the decision had been taken after a survey in August found that roughly a third of the two-tonne units on the seaward face had shifted or cracked. "We can either close now and finish before the January gales, or wait and risk losing the whole structure," said Marta Keel, the authority's chief engineer, at a public meeting in the town hall.

The ferry operator will run a reduced service from the old quay on the western side of the harbour, adding around twenty minutes to each crossing. Fishing vessels will be allocated berths at Coldwater Basin, six kilometres up the coast, and the authority will pay for a shuttle bus for crews.

Marina owners were less satisfied. The leisure berths cannot be relocated, and boat owners have been told to lift out or move their vessels to other ports at their own expense. A residents' group has asked the council to press for compensation.

The works are budgeted at four point two million and are funded jointly by the regional government and the harbour authority's own reserves. A second phase, covering the northern arm, is expected to follow in 2028 once funding is confirmed.
