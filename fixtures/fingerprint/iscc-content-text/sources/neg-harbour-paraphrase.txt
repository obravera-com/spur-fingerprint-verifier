Beginning on 1 October, no vessels will be able to pass the eastern breakwater at Port Meridian for a period of eleven weeks. The port's governing body announced the decision on Tuesday, explaining that the concrete blocks protecting the structure, which were badly knocked about during the storms of last winter, must be replaced. Those who use the fishing harbour, the Salt Island ferry and the little yacht basin sheltered by the breakwater will all feel the effects.

According to the port's management, an inspection carried out in August showed that about one in three of the heavy protective blocks on the exposed side had either moved or fractured. Speaking to residents gathered at the town hall, chief engineer Marta Keel put the choice starkly: shut down immediately and complete the job ahead of the winter gales, or delay and risk the breakwater failing altogether.

Ferry passengers will be served by a cut-down timetable running from the old landing stage on the harbour's western shore, which lengthens every trip by roughly a third of an hour. The fishing boats are being moved to Coldwater Basin, some six kilometres along the coast, with the port paying for a bus to carry crews back and forth.

Yacht owners have fared worse. Their moorings cannot be moved elsewhere, so they must either haul their boats out of the water or find berths in other harbours, paying the cost themselves. A local residents' association has written to the council urging it to seek financial redress on their behalf.

The project carries a price tag of £4.2 million, shared between the regional administration and the port's own funds. Repairs to the northern arm of the breakwater are pencilled in for 2028, subject to money being found.
